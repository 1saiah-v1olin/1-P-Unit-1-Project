My Favorite Music🎼🎹

Isaiah Brown

My favorite genre of music is 1000% classical music. I listen to other kinds of music, but my playlists are mostly classical.
My favorite composer that I played over the summer is definitely Mussorgsky's Pictures at an Exhibition. Mussorgsky was born in Russia on March 21, 1839, and was one of the defining composers of the Romantic period.
One of Mussorgsky's most famous pieces is Pictures at an Exhibition, originally written for piano, but wonderfully orchestrated by Maurice Ravel in 1922, many years after Mussorgsky's death. Mussorgsky had written this piece while grieving his late friend, Viktor Hartmann, who was a painter. Mussorgsky walked through an exhibition of Hartmann's paintings and was inspired to create music through the paintings. The piece has 10 movements, with 6 themes, known as Promenades, in between certain pieces to link them together, depicting Mussorgsky himself walking through the exhibition of paintings.

  1. The first movement, Gnomus, depicts a crooked-legged, clumsy gnome stumbling around, shown by the occasional orchestral hits symbolizing the gnome tripping and falling. The orchestration of low strings playing what sounds like random notes shows how the gnome is almost incapable of walking normally.
  2. The 2nd movement, Il Vecchio Castello, depicts an old castle and a troubador nearby singing a song. The troubador in this case is an alto saxophone, which has a great mix of warmth and airy quality to its sound. 🎶🏰
  3. The 3rd movement, Tuileries, depicts children teasing their nannies in the famous Tuileries Garden of France. The light-hearted, almost mocking sound of the oboes and flutes paints the image perfectly. 🧒
  4. The 4th movement, Bydlo, is about a giant Polish oxcart being dragged through mud. Probably the tuba's most favorite solo in the classical repertoire, it is depicting the ox, and the low strings and low woodwinds are the huge wheels dragging through the mud. The entire movement is one big crescendo and decrescendo, showing the true size of this ox. 🐮
  5. The 5th movement, The Ballet of Unhatched Chicks, is about chicks that haven't fully hatched from their eggs yet. Hartmann was tasked to design costumes for a children's ballet titled Trilby, depicting canary chicks emerging from their shells. The orchestration is light-hearted and almost skittish, with the high woodwinds' delicate grace notes and string pizzicato mimicking chirping and peeping movements. 🐣
  6. The 6th movement, Samuel Goldenberg and Schmuyle, depicts two Jews, one rich and one poor. The richer of the two, Samuel Goldenberg, has a grand entrance backed by strings and bass clarinet. On the other hand, Schmuyle's entrance is almost pitiful and pathetic, backed by a muted piccolo trumpet and sad, whining woodwinds. 💰
  7. The 7th movement, Limoges, the Market, depicts women arguing violently over breaking news. The constant, almost panicking tone of the strings as well as the woodwinds mimics heated conversation. 😡💢
  8. The 8th movement,Catacombs (Catacombae: Sepulcrum romanum), portrays the painter and a guide exploring the Roman Catacombs in Paris, its walls lined with skulls. This movement is directly linked to the previous movement, marked as attacca, meaning "to join" or "to bind together". The low brass are orchestrated in deep, warm chords, giving the movement a dark, ghostly atmosphere. The interlude between this movement and the next, Cum mortuis in lingua mortua, or "with/among the dead in a dead language", depicts the composer having a conversation with his late friend in Latin. ☠️
  9. The 9th movement, "La Cabane sur des pattes de poule" or "The Hut on Fowl's Legs", depicts the hut of Baba Yaga, a folkloric witch who flies around a forest in a mortar and eats lost children, on two chicken legs. The movement starts suddenly after the quiet fade of the previous interlude, depicting Baba Yaga discovering a child, and starting a frantic chase backed by low brass and low strings. The middle section of this movement is much quieter, showing how the child was able to momentarily get away from the evil witch. The atmosphere is thick with tension, as the child wanders around, trying to escape the forest, while Baba Yaga and her hut are still looking for the child. The main theme returns with no warning, portraying the child being found by the witch and her hut, and the chase continues, and one final build up leads to the next movement.
  10. The 10th and final movement, La grande porte de Kiew or "The Great Gate of Kiev", is about an architectural design for a massive gate in the city of Kiev in Ukraine. The movement is also attacca like the transition between movements 7 and 8, meaning the final build up from Baba Yaga goes straight into this grand movement. There are several sections of this movement, from grand fanfares to solemn chorales in the winds.
